# I Love My Body — Content and Product Blueprint

Status: content freeze before frontend implementation  
Product: evidence-mapped, non-invasive online hospital  
Core promise: **Happiness now. Illness never.**

## 1. What I Love My Body is

I Love My Body is an evidence-mapped, non-invasive online hospital designed to work alongside a person’s existing medical treatment.

It begins with the person—not only an illness, report or medicine. It brings medical information into one connected view with thoughts, beliefs, feelings, instincts, relationships, intimacy, lifestyle, work, dreams, body signals and daily choices.

The hospital does not replace doctors, diagnose from an algorithm, prescribe medicines, or tell anyone to stop treatment. Medical decisions remain between the person and appropriately qualified clinicians.

Its role is to:

- gather a clear starting picture;
- help the person notice connections in their own life;
- provide sourced explanations where evidence exists;
- offer non-invasive practices and guided experiments;
- record what the person chooses and experiences;
- compare change over time;
- help the person and their clinicians make better-informed decisions.

## 2. The philosophy

### Primary line

**Happiness now. Illness never.**

This is an intention and a direction—not a promised medical outcome.

### Personal foundation

> My body is my present.  
> I care for my body because I am alive now.  
> I love my body and I love my life.

### Connected model

Everything is connected.

Physical, sexual, gut, emotional, relational, mental and occupational experience do not sit around a person as separate boxes. They are lived together, within one person.

**Instinct is the centre. Love is the lever. Action is the accelerator.**

The internal mathematics and formulas support the platform’s analysis. They are not the marketing headline and do not need to be shown to the participant.

## 3. Who may enter

The website offers three clear entry paths. They are not separate brands or hospitals.

### Prevention and early support

For someone who is alive, values life and wants to understand and care for their body now. This may include people noticing stress, worry, low mood, low confidence, dependence patterns, changing blood pressure, reduced stamina or other early concerns.

### Alongside an ongoing illness

For someone with a diagnosed illness who wants to understand the wider context around their medical reports, medicines, body signals, thoughts, feelings, food, relationships, routines and actions—without changing medical treatment through the platform.

### Post-operative and recovery support

For someone recovering after an operation or medical treatment who wants a connected record of reports, prescriptions, symptoms, energy, feelings, actions, support and daily life.

## 4. What the hospital offers

### A private starting place

- create a personal profile;
- record existing conditions and important history;
- upload current and earlier reports;
- upload prescriptions;
- photograph medicine labels and packaging;
- confirm the medicine name, strength and instructions;
- record operations, allergies and safety information;
- choose what the person wants to understand.

### PRE-10: ten days to complete clarity

PRE-10 is information gathering before treatment begins. It is not a food diary and it is not a ten-day challenge.

It uses short, saveable sessions across ten connected dimensions:

1. Physical and medical
2. Thoughts and attention
3. Beliefs and meaning
4. Feelings and expression
5. Personality and values
6. Lifestyle and choices
7. Relationships and belonging
8. Intimacy, sexuality and desire
9. Work, dreams and direction
10. Instinct, courage and self-trust

The movement is:

**Cluttered → confused → comfort zone → clean → clear → complete clarity**

No dimension receives a “good person” or “bad person” score. The result is a participant-owned **Complete Clarity Map** made of:

- information supplied;
- strengths and supports noticed;
- connections to explore;
- differences or tensions the participant notices;
- information still missing;
- the participant’s own words and priorities.

### The N.A.I.V.E. journey

After PRE-10, the participant may begin the existing N.A.I.V.E. journey:

- seven weeks;
- reassessment;
- a second seven weeks only if it is appropriate and chosen.

This is not a fixed 100-day challenge.

The journey helps a participant notice, allow, investigate, voice and engage with their experience through structured daily and weekly activities. The exact stage language is stored in the backend and must be rendered from data, not re-invented in frontend copy.

### Daily “listen to my body”

Daily check-in remains gentle and optional. More information can improve pattern-finding, but no ordinary answer is compulsory.

Possible check-ins include:

- mood and feelings, whenever they arise;
- sleep, energy, calm, stress and connection;
- pain or discomfort and where it is felt;
- cravings;
- hunger, thirst and satisfaction;
- burping, gas, vomiting or other digestive signals;
- stool colour, form and ease;
- urine colour and relevant change;
- bathing, brushing, clothing and personal cleanliness;
- movement, rest and environment;
- food, including an optional photograph later in the journey;
- medicines taken as prescribed;
- what happened before;
- where, when, why, how and with whom;
- what action the person chose;
- what seemed to happen afterwards.

A photograph may estimate food and quantity, but the participant must always be able to correct the identified food, preparation, portion and certainty. A photograph is an estimate, never a fact.

### Reports over time

The platform may compare:

- medical reports and measurements;
- medicine records;
- validated assessment results;
- participant check-ins;
- actions and practices;
- body signals;
- time, context and completeness.

The platform shows observations and sourced explanations. It does not decide that a medicine is no longer needed. A person may hope for any outcome; changes to medicine remain a clinical decision.

## 5. Assessments

### Two clearly separated classes

#### Validated instruments

These retain their official wording, scoring, recall period, source and limitations.

Currently connected:

- WHO-5 Well-Being Index;
- PHQ-9, conditional and with a safety workflow;
- GAD-7, conditional;
- a frozen public-domain IPIP personality scale;
- Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, optional.

A score is never a diagnosis or an identity. The interface says what the instrument measures, where it came from and what the participant may wish to discuss with a professional.

#### I Love My Body discovery assessments

These are original question sets. They do not claim clinical validation.

They explore:

- present reality;
- thought and attention patterns;
- beliefs and the person’s relationship with their body;
- feelings and expression;
- values and lived alignment;
- lifestyle and comfort zones;
- relationships and belonging;
- intimacy, touch, consent and desire;
- dreams, work and direction;
- instinct, courage, self-confidence and self-trust;
- complete clarity.

Important distinctions:

- **Self-worth:** “Do I experience myself as valuable?”
- **Self-confidence:** “Do I believe I can handle or learn this?”
- **Courage:** “What may I choose even while fear is present?”

These constructs must never be collapsed into one score.

## 6. Our voice

### We ask; we do not judge

Use:

- “What do you notice?”
- “Which words feel closest today?”
- “Would you like to explore this?”
- “What seems to happen before or after?”
- “What would feel supportive?”
- “You may skip this.”
- “Something else—in your own words.”
- “This is one possible connection to explore.”

Do not use:

- “You are…”
- “Your personality type is…”
- “Your problem is…”
- “This proves…”
- “You failed…”
- “Good food / bad food”
- “Compliant / non-compliant”
- “Normal person / abnormal person”
- labels inferred from a questionnaire;
- blame, shame, guilt or fear as motivation.

### Optimistic, not falsely reassuring

Optimism means:

- beginning with what is working;
- explaining what information means calmly;
- distinguishing an urgent signal from a frightening interpretation;
- showing what can be understood or acted on;
- acknowledging uncertainty;
- respecting medical safety.

Optimism does not mean hiding an urgent result, promising a cure or declaring that every value is perfect.

### Praise and feedback

Feedback may motivate, validate, remind and excite. It must praise the action—not define the person.

Good:

- “You paused and noticed. That gives us useful context.”
- “You added a report today. Your starting picture is becoming clearer.”
- “You chose your own words. They belong in your map.”
- “Enough for today is a complete choice.”

Avoid:

- “Perfect patient.”
- “You were good today.”
- “You are cured.”
- “This action fixed your body.”

## 7. Evidence and sources

Every participant-facing explanation must be able to show:

- source title;
- source organisation or authors;
- source type;
- publication or release year where available;
- direct URL or identifier;
- what the source supports;
- what it does not establish;
- review status;
- date accessed;
- applicability note.

Evidence badges:

- **Official or regulatory**
- **Validated instrument**
- **Clinical study**
- **Systematic review**
- **Mechanistic evidence**
- **Traditional framework**
- **Author philosophy**
- **Participant observation**
- **Estimate**

Traditional practices, philosophy and scientific findings must not be presented as if they are the same evidence type.

The backend already stores references and connections across food chemistry, pathology, pharmacy, clinical psychology, neuroscience, medical formulas, traditional non-invasive frameworks and the author’s audited book content. The frontend must retrieve this provenance; it must never invent a citation.

## 8. Complementary approaches

The platform may introduce approaches the founder has personally experienced or deliberately selected, such as:

- food chemistry and cooking;
- breath and relaxation practices;
- movement;
- counselling and psychotherapy;
- pranic healing;
- acupuncture and acupressure;
- Ayurveda;
- yoga;
- meditation;
- other non-invasive practices added after review.

Each approach page must separate:

1. what the tradition or practitioner proposes;
2. what the available research supports;
3. what remains uncertain;
4. safety boundaries and when medical review is required;
5. the founder’s personal experience, clearly identified as personal experience.

Do not call practitioners “magicians” at launch.

Use **Practitioners & Guides**. Each person may have a compact microsite only after founder selection and verification of qualifications, scope, location, availability and claims.

## 9. Website information architecture

Keep the public navigation small:

1. **Home**
2. **How it works**
3. **Evidence**
4. **Explore**
5. **About**
6. **My Happy Space** — primary action

“Explore” contains:

- complementary approaches;
- practitioners and guides;
- stories and founder-selected resources;
- book and journal content.

The seven responsive views are screen-size states, not seven required navigation tabs:

1. compact phone;
2. standard phone;
3. large phone / small foldable;
4. tablet portrait;
5. tablet landscape;
6. laptop;
7. wide desktop.

## 11. Frontend restraint

The visual design begins only after this content is accepted in code.

Rules:

- preserve the soothing animated background;
- use substantially smaller headings;
- one main idea per viewport;
- no oversized shouting typography;
- short line lengths;
- generous empty space;
- one primary action at a time;
- no permanent vertical rail on phones;
- never show all programme details in the hero;
- progressive disclosure for sensitive questions;
- save and continue later;
- clear progress without pressure;
- participant-controlled colour and type preferences inside My Happy Space;
- every state must work at all seven responsive widths;
- reduced-motion and high-contrast support are mandatory.

## 12. Non-negotiable safety and privacy

- explicit consent and withdrawal;
- sensitive questions optional;
- prefer-not-to-answer is a valid response;
- urgent safety responses do not receive automated cheerful messages;
- personal records are private;
- public stories require separate explicit consent and de-identification;
- no automated medicine change;
- no diagnosis by formula;
- no practitioner may exceed their verified scope;
- no source badge without a stored source;
- no health claim based only on philosophy, tradition or testimony.
